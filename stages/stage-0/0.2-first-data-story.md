# 0.2 First data story

Week 2. About 10 hours.

Your first project is small on purpose: one question about your own community, one dataset, one chart, and about 300 words. The aim is to finish something real, end to end, and publish it.

By the end of this module you can:

- Find a public dataset and record where it came from and its license
- Load a CSV file with pandas and make one clear chart
- Write a short, honest story about what the chart shows and what it does not

## Choosing your question and data

Pick something you care about in your city, region, or country. Keep the question small enough to answer with one chart. Examples:

- How has the share of the population living in cities changed in my country since 1990?
- Which months have the most rainfall where I live?
- How does school enrolment for girls and boys compare in my region?

Good places to look for data:

- Your national statistics office
- World Bank Open Data (data.worldbank.org)
- Our World in Data (ourworldindata.org)
- The Humanitarian Data Exchange (data.humdata.org)
- WHO Global Health Observatory

Rules for your data:

- It must be public, and you must record its source and license.
- Download it as a CSV file and commit it in the `data` folder. Keep each file under 5 MB.
- No personal data about individuals.

## Assignment: first data story

Accept the **first data story** assignment from the link shared in the forum, then open `story.ipynb` in Colab. It has five sections. Replace every `TODO`:

| Section | What to write |
|---|---|
| Question | One sentence. The question your chart answers. |
| Data | `Source:` a link to where the data came from. `License:` its license, for example CC BY 4.0. One or two sentences on what the data covers. |
| Chart | Code that loads your CSV from the `data` folder and draws one chart, with a title, axis labels, and units. |
| Story | 200 to 400 words: what the chart shows, what surprised you, and at least one thing the data cannot tell us. |
| Reflection | Two sentences: what was hardest, and what you would do next with more time. |

Save the notebook back to GitHub (File, Save a copy in GitHub) and check the Actions tab. Every automatic check must pass.

## What the automatic checks look for

- Every section is filled in, with no `TODO` left
- The Data section has a source link and a license
- The Story has 200 to 400 words
- The notebook runs from top to bottom without errors
- It produces at least one chart
- Data files are in `data/` and under 5 MB each
- No access keys or passwords anywhere in the repository

The checks confirm the work is complete. Whether it is good is what peer review is for.

## Peer review

After you submit, review two peers' stories using the [peer review guide](peer-review.md). You will receive two reviews in return. Make at least one improvement based on your reviews before the end of the week.

## Publishing

Stories that pass their checks and reviews are published on the Data Insight website. Instructions for opening your publishing pull request are shared at the end of the week.
